## Executive Summary

The Elvato storefront has strong brand foundations — a distinctive visual identity (torn receipt cards, crinkle overlays, mono typography) and a clear value proposition for lighting buyers. However, the main page is currently in a **mid-development state** with significant placeholder content, missing SEO metadata, production debug logs throughout, and no control over category display order or count. The highest-impact improvements are replacing placeholder content, controlling collection ordering, and adding trust/social proof elements that lighting buyers expect.

## Section-by-Section Analysis

### 1. Hero Section
**File:** `src/modules/home/components/hero/index.tsx`

- **Current state:** Split 50/50 layout — left panel with headline, subtext, category badges, and two CTAs; right panel with hero image and 5 featured product thumbnails. Strong headline: "Contemporary lighting for your next project." Good badge row with category quick-links.
- **Issues:**
  - **Category badges are not linked** — the `<Button variant="link">` elements in the badge row don't have `href` props. They render as non-functional buttons. A customer clicking "chandeliers" expects to go somewhere.
  - **Badge order is suboptimal** — current order: `pendants, chandeliers, ceiling, wall, desk & floor, controls, sale`. Chandeliers should lead (highest AOV/visual impact), or "Featured" / "New Arrivals" should come first.
  - **"DESIGN SERVICES" CTA links to `/store`** — this is a dead end. It should link to a dedicated services/contact page or the Secondary Hero's controls section.
  - **Featured thumbnails link to `#`** — the 5 product squares in the hero right panel all have `href="#"`. These need to link to actual featured products or collections.
  - **"COUPON / SAVE15" ticker is hidden** (`className="hidden"`) — if this is intended for promotions, it should either be activated or removed.
  - **Alt text is generic** — all featured images use `alt="Featured"` instead of descriptive product names.
  - **Inline styles** — `style={{ background: 'linear-gradient(...)' }}` should be Tailwind classes or CSS modules for consistency.
- **Recommendations:**
  1. Wire up category badges as `<LocalizedClientLink>` elements pointing to `/categories/{handle}` or `/store?category={handle}`
  2. Reorder badges: Featured → Chandeliers → Pendants → Ceiling → Wall → Desk & Floor → Controls → Sale
  3. Link "DESIGN SERVICES" to a dedicated page (`/design-services` or `/contact`)
  4. Link featured thumbnails to actual product PDPs or collection pages
  5. Add a trust signal line below the CTAs (e.g., "Trusted by 500+ designers" or star rating)
- **Priority:** **Critical** (non-functional navigation elements)

### 5. Featured Products / Product Rails
**Files:** `src/modules/home/components/featured-products/index.tsx`, `src/modules/home/components/featured-products/product-rail/index.tsx`

- **Current state:** Renders ALL collections from Medusa in whatever order the API returns them. Each collection shows as a horizontal rail with a title, "View all" link, and 6-column grid of product cards + 1 ad card.
- **Issues:**
  - **No control over collection order or count** — `FeaturedProducts` iterates over every collection from `listCollections()`. There's no filtering, sorting, or limit. If you have 15 collections, all 15 render on the homepage.
  - **No limit on products per rail** — the query fetches ALL products in a collection with no `limit` param. A collection with 200 products would attempt to render 200 cards in a single rail.
  - **"Ad Space" / "Your Ad Here" placeholder card** is appended to every rail — this is placeholder content that needs real content or removal.
  - **6-column grid is aggressive** — on most screens, 6 small cards may be too compressed. Consider 4-5 with a "see more" scroll or limiting to first 8 products.
  - **Console.log statements** in ProductRail — 3 debug logs per collection rail that will fire in production.
  - **No "Shop by Room"** section — lighting buyers often browse by room (kitchen, bathroom, living room, bedroom, outdoor) rather than fixture type.
- **Recommendations:**
  1. **Control collection order explicitly** — either hardcode a priority list of collection handles or add a `sort_order` metadata field in Medusa, then filter/sort in the page component. Recommended order: Featured → Chandeliers → Pendants → Ceiling → Wall → Floor & Table → Outdoor → Controls
  2. Limit products per rail to 5-8 with a `limit` query param
  3. Limit total rails on homepage to 4-6 collections max (not all)
  4. Remove the "Ad Space" placeholder card or replace with a promotional/editorial card
  5. Add horizontal scroll for product rails on mobile instead of wrapping
  6. Remove console.log statements
- **Priority:** **Critical** (this is the core merchandising section)

| Position | Collection | Why |

| — | *Editorial Break: "Shop by Room" or "Editor's Picks" grid* | |

## Strategic Recommendations

These require design/development effort but would significantly elevate the storefront:

1. **Implement collection ordering & limiting** — Add a mechanism (hardcoded priority list or Medusa metadata) to control which collections appear on the homepage and in what order. Cap product rails at 6-8 products with a `limit` param.

2. **Build a category mega-menu** — The nav "COLLECTIONS" link should trigger a dropdown showing all categories with small thumbnail images. This is standard for lighting e-commerce and critical for discovery.

3. **Add "Shop by Room" section** — Create a visual grid section (replacing the current placeholder ProductGrid) with 4-6 room categories (Living Room, Kitchen, Bedroom, Bathroom, Dining, Outdoor), each with a lifestyle image. Lighting buyers frequently start with "I need a light for my kitchen."

4. **Enrich product cards** — Add star ratings, quick-view, sale badges, and color swatch dots to the ProductPreview component. These are conversion multipliers on product impression surfaces.

5. **Add social proof** — An Instagram feed section, customer reviews carousel, or "As seen in..." press logos section would build significant trust. For lighting, visual social proof (real installation photos) is extremely powerful.

6. **Newsletter / email capture** — Add a newsletter signup section (mid-page or footer) with a lighting-specific hook: "Get design inspiration & exclusive offers" with an email input.

7. **Build the "DESIGN SERVICES" and trade program pages** — The Secondary Hero promotes services that don't have landing pages yet. Professional buyers (designers, contractors) are a high-value segment.
